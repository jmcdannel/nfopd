What NFOPD matches, beats and still lacks

NFOPD already covers what OpenSnow sells as Base and Premium forecasting: 10-day detail, days 11–15, multi-model comparison and forecast confidence. It's free, with no login. OpenSnow still leads on human forecasters, snowfall maps, push alerts, app-store apps, and webcams outside the West.

Where NFOPD is ahead

  • It shows its work. Every number names its source (resort, SNOTEL or model), and the model table shows each forecast that goes into the blend.
  • Real odds. The chance of 1", 6" and 12"+ comes from 82 ensemble runs, and the confidence score is a number you can compare day to day.
  • A track record. NFOPD scores 30 days of past forecasts against what SNOTEL gauges measured, so you know how far to trust them at your mountain.
  • NOAA first. The official forecast, hourly grid, snow level and forecaster discussion sit next to the models, with nothing behind a paywall.

Gaps to close next

  • Push alerts. A morning powder alert when a favorite resort reports or forecasts 6"+.
  • Cams beyond the West. Only 18 resorts outside the West have cams. East of the Rockies NFOPD lists just the 66 areas with 1,000 ft or more of vertical.
  • Snowfall maps. Radar, snow depth and snowfall odds are layers now, and each forecast page has a satellite map of its forecast point. A forecast snowfall-amount map and weather-satellite images are still missing.
  • Human forecasters. NFOPD Daily is AI-written, and Idaho, Montana, the Southwest, Alaska and BC have no free independent forecaster to link. OpenSnow pays people to write these every day.
  • Past seasons. History covers this season only, and it's modeled, not measured.
  • Official forecasts abroad. Resorts outside the US and Canada get models only: no national weather service or avalanche bulletin is read yet.
  • Scoring NFOPD's own blend over time. This needs server-side storage, such as a nightly Vercel job writing to Redis or Blob storage.
